# Crestline Cloud · Revenue Funnel

A revenue funnel analytics template for a B2B SaaS sales motion (annual contracts, seats plus consumption credits), covering both halves of the motion: a lead funnel (created → qualified → meeting held → sales-qualified) and a deal funnel (created → discovery → evaluation → proposal → negotiation → closed). It ships four views — **Funnel**, **Trends**, **Closed-lost** and **Sources** — sharing one global filter bar (segment / source / week range). The **Funnel** view renders both halves as two full-width proportional funnels drawn in hand-rolled SVG: each stage is a horizontally centred band whose width is exactly proportional to that stage's intake, consecutive bands are joined by a tapering connector that represents precisely the advancing cohort (`advanced_N === intake_{N+1}`, verified and logged once per funnel), and the width that does not continue leaves sideways as outflow wings — still-pending exits right, dropped exits left, subdivided on the deal funnel into slivers by loss-reason category. A derived lineage connector sits between the two funnels, stating how many sales-qualified leads opened a deal record and showing deals that arrive with no lead lineage as a labelled side-inflow into the deal funnel's first band rather than pretending the two totals reconcile. An ARR-weighted toggle re-scales the deal funnel's geometry by pipeline ARR instead of record count, wings included. The one disclosed concession to legibility is a documented minimum render width: bands are floored at a fraction of the widest band so late stages keep their label block, which never changes a printed number and is stated in every stage explainer. The signature interaction is unchanged — clicking any band splits its intake into *advanced*, *still pending* and *dropped*, with the dropped band decomposed by loss-reason category down to the individual records. Every tile and chart carries a "how this is computed" popover that states its formula and thresholds, so a reviewer never has to guess what a number means.

A lead is a **person at a company**, not the company itself: each record carries `contactName`, `contactTitle` and a coarse `seniority` bucket, with `companyName` as an attribute of that contact, and the seniority mix skews senior upmarket. Dropped leads carry a categorical `disqualReason` that mirrors the deal side's `LossReason` — a fixed union (`not_icp`, `wrong_persona`, `support_request`, `no_budget`, `competitor`, `timing`, `unresponsive`, `junk`) with a fixed per-category detail list, scoped per stage so a pre-qualification drop offers different reasons than a no-show after a booked meeting. Reasons only ever explain a drop; disposition stays derived from milestone gaps. Drops with no recorded reason are pooled as "Reason not recorded" so the reason parts always sum back to the stage's dropped count (asserted and logged), which is what licenses the lead funnel's dropped wings subdividing by reason and the drill-down going band → reason → individual people.

Crestline Cloud is fictional and every company, contact, deal and loss reason is synthetic, generated deterministically from a fixed seed (mulberry32) so server render and client hydration match exactly. Swapping in real data means replacing the `LEADS` and `DEALS` arrays in `src/lib/rf/data.ts` with records conforming to the `Lead` and `Deal` interfaces (a `Lead` is a contact plus their account, with an optional categorical `disqualReason`); weeks are integer indices `0 .. CURRENT_WEEK` measured from `WINDOW_START_ISO` (the Monday of week 0), and a milestone that has not happened is stored as `null` rather than a future week. Architecturally, every figure on screen derives at runtime from those two arrays in `src/lib/rf/engine.ts` — nothing is authored twice — and the derivations reconcile by construction: the disposition bands are exhaustive so they always sum to stage intake, and the closed-lost matrix cells always sum to the headline lost ARR across all three groupings (asserted, and logged to the console). Stage definitions are editable configs (`LEAD_STAGES` / `DEAL_STAGES`), so a different funnel shape needs no page edits — add, remove or rename a stage and all four views follow, including the derived cohort-maturity cutoffs.

The **Trends** view leads with plain weekly counts — leads created, leads qualified, SQOs, deals created, deals won — one metric per chart on a weekly axis, each against a target reference line. Targets are the only authored input besides the records themselves (`WEEKLY_TARGETS` in `src/lib/rf/data.ts`, one number per series); attainment against them is derived in `engine.ts`. A weekly count is final once its week closes, so those charts carry no maturity treatment; the dashed-line / hollow-marker / shaded-band convention is reserved for rates and cohort shares, which is where it belongs. The cohort analysis is still there, demoted below the counts and renamed **cohort conversion**: of the leads created in week N, what share later reached qualified, booked a meeting, reached sales-qualified or went on to win.

## Bringing your own data

Everything on screen derives from two arrays, so swapping in real numbers means replacing `LEADS` and `DEALS` in `src/lib/rf/data.ts` with records conforming to the `Lead` and `Deal` interfaces — no component or derivation changes. The intended no-code path is the Lovable chat: remix the template, attach your CRM export, and ask Lovable to map your columns onto those interfaces. The in-app **Use your data** page walks through it step by step, including ready-to-paste prompts for swapping the data, renaming funnel stages in `LEAD_STAGES` / `DEAL_STAGES`, and rebranding. Weeks are integer indices `0 .. CURRENT_WEEK` from `WINDOW_START_ISO`, and a milestone that hasn't happened is `null` rather than a future week. The seeded sample dataset stays the zero-configuration default, and the console reconciliation log tells you immediately whether your own records hold the same invariants.

## Development

```sh
npm i
npm run dev
```

Built with TanStack Start, TypeScript, React and Tailwind CSS. Charts are hand-rolled SVG primitives in `src/components/rf/Chart.tsx` and the funnel diagram in `src/components/rf/FunnelShape.tsx` — no chart library.
